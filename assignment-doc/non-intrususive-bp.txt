Introduction/Challenge:
The war against gravity is an ongoing battle in which your body is constantly fighting to maintain cerebral blood flow. Sustaining adequate blood pressures is key to winning this war. Undertaken in several ways, ranging from elevate synaptic flow of neurotransmitters in the autonomic nervous system, to simple leg manoeuvres which you perform when walking. Maintaining adequate blood pressure is vital to homeostasis. The most common instrument used to measure blood pressure is the sphygmomanometer, or the blood pressure cuff. A sphygmomanometer comprises of an inflatable cuff used to constrain blood flow and a mercury manometer to measure pressure. Typically used conjunction with a stethoscope, the sphygmomanometer, occludes blood flow when inflated, and when deflated, lets the blood flow freely. During occlusion a thumping sound occurs as the vessel collapses. When the pressure is released from the sphygmomanometer, and blood flow returns, a tapping sound emanates from the vein. These two “Korotkoff” sounds, represent what is commonly referred to as systolic and diastolic blood pressure, and hence the reason Blood pressure is always recorded as two numbers - i.e., 120/80. While sphygmomanometers are regarded as the gold standard instruments for measuring blood pressure, they cannot be used continuously for fear of restricting blood flow. Consequently, the development of an applicable model for non-invasive monitoring of blood pressure for a more comprehensive array of patients would be beneficial. This project requires you to develop a non-intrusive means for continuously interpolating blood pressure. Your proposed model should revolve around a term called pulse transient time. The model holds an inverse relationship between blood pressure and the time between the peak of the electrical activity in the heart and the peak volume of blood in your left fingers. 

Abstract:
This assignment is entirely desk based, organised around a specific machine learning task. It is intended to give students an opportunity to integrate and expand their knowledge in several different core areas: MatLab, Machine learning, algorithm development, software development and AI. This is achieved by designing, building, debugging, and demonstrating a complete functional predictive model to continuous interpolate blood pressure. 

The objective is to design and prototype a machine learning framework to predict one’s blood pressure. The dataset containing 11 subjects ECG (QRST Function) and PPG signals, with blood pressure measurements taken in the supine, orthostatic and fowler positions. Addition marks will be provided if the framework can identify both systolic and diastolic blood pressure or a biomechanical reason as to why only one measurement can be predicted.

6. Results (6%)
